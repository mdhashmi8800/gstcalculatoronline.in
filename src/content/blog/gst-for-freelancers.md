## GST Rules for Freelancers and Independent Consultants in India

India's freelance economy has grown exponentially, with millions of software developers, designers, writers, consultants, and content creators working independently. Understanding your Goods and Services Tax (GST) obligations is not optional — it's a legal requirement once you cross certain turnover thresholds, and it unlocks significant financial benefits like ITC claims and zero-rated export treatment.

This guide covers everything a freelancer needs to know about GST in 2026: registration thresholds, invoicing rules, export treatment, ITC eligibility, return filing obligations, and common compliance mistakes to avoid.

---

## 1. GST Registration Threshold for Freelancers

Unlike goods traders (threshold: ₹40 lakh), freelancers are **service providers** and face lower registration thresholds:

| Type of Freelancer | Mandatory Registration Threshold |
|---|---|
| Services within regular states | ₹20 lakh per year |
| Services in special category states | ₹10 lakh per year |
| Export of services to foreign clients | Any turnover (mandatory) |
| Services through e-commerce platforms | Any turnover (mandatory) |

**Special category states** (lower ₹10 lakh threshold): Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh.

> **Important:** "Aggregate turnover" means the total value of all taxable, exempt, and zero-rated supplies across all business activities in India — even if from multiple clients. Do not just count a single client's payments.

---

## 2. How GST Applies to Freelancing Income

When you provide services to a client in India, you charge GST based on the **Place of Supply** rules:

### Domestic Clients (India)
- **Same state as you (Intra-state):** Charge CGST + SGST at 18% (9% each)
- **Different state from you (Inter-state):** Charge IGST at 18%

### International Clients (Exports)
Services exported to foreign clients are classified as **zero-rated supplies** under Section 16 of the IGST Act. This means:
- You charge 0% GST on your invoice to the foreign client
- You must file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) online at the GST portal at the start of each financial year
- You can claim a refund of any ITC accumulated on your business inputs

**Example:**  
A Bangalore-based UI/UX designer completes a ₹5,00,000 design project for a UK company:
- With LUT filed: Invoice shows ₹5,00,000 with 0% IGST
- Without LUT: Invoice shows ₹5,00,000 + ₹90,000 IGST (18%), then claim IGST refund

## 3. GST Invoicing Requirements for Freelancers

As a registered freelancer, every supply of service must be supported by a valid **Tax Invoice** containing:

1. GSTIN of the supplier (your GST number)
2. Invoice number (sequential, financial year-based)
3. Invoice date
4. Name, address, and GSTIN of the recipient (if B2B)
5. Description of the service
6. SAC (Services Accounting Code)
7. Taxable value
8. CGST + SGST rate and amount (intra-state) OR IGST rate and amount (inter-state/exports)
9. Total invoice value

> **Invoicing Deadline:** You must issue a tax invoice within **30 days** of completing the service (or from the date of receipt of payment, whichever is earlier).

## 5. Input Tax Credit (ITC) for Freelancers

One of the major financial benefits of GST registration is the ability to claim **Input Tax Credit** — a reduction in your GST liability by the amount of GST you paid on your business purchases.

### What Can Freelancers Claim as ITC?
- **Laptops, computers, tablets:** GST paid on purchase
- **Office furniture and equipment:** GST paid (if used for business)
- **Software subscriptions:** Adobe, Figma, GitHub, Slack (18% GST paid = claimable as ITC)
- **Internet and broadband bills:** GST component is claimable
- **Professional courses and training:** If used for business purposes
- **Office rent (if GST is charged by landlord):** Claimable

### What Cannot Be Claimed?
- Personal expenses (home internet for personal use)
- Mobile phones used partly for personal use (only business portion)
- Motor vehicles (with certain exceptions)
- Food and beverages

> **ITC Matching:** Your supplier's GST returns (GSTR-1/GSTR-2A) must match what you claim. Unmatched ITC can be rejected.

## 6. GST Return Filing Obligations

Once registered, freelancers must file the following returns:

| Return | Due Date | What It Contains |
|---|---|---|
| GSTR-1 | 11th of next month (monthly) | All outward sales invoices |
| GSTR-3B | 20th of next month (monthly) | Summary of sales, ITC, and net tax payable |
| GSTR-9 | 31st December | Annual return (if turnover > ₹2 crore) |

**Composition Scheme Option:**  
If your annual turnover is below ₹50 lakh, you can opt for the **Composition Scheme for Service Providers** and pay a flat 6% tax quarterly without filing monthly returns. However, you cannot claim ITC under this scheme and cannot export services.

## 7. Practical GST Scenarios for Freelancers

### Scenario 1: Domestic Freelancer (Software Developer, Mumbai)
**Annual Turnover:** ₹35,00,000 (domestic clients only)
- Must register (exceeds ₹20 lakh threshold)
- Charges 18% GST: CGST 9% + SGST 9% for Maharashtra clients
- Charges IGST 18% for clients in other states
- Claims ITC on laptop (₹1,20,000 × 18% = ₹21,600 claimable)
- Files GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B monthly

### Scenario 2: Export-Focused Freelancer (Designer, Pune serving UK clients)
**Annual Turnover:** ₹12,00,000 (100% from UK client)
- Must register (mandatory for exporters regardless of turnover)
- Files LUT online at start of financial year
- Issues invoices at 0% GST
- Claims full ITC refund on business purchases
- Files NIL GSTR-3B (as outward tax is 0%)

### Scenario 3: Mixed Client Base (Consultant, Bengaluru)
**Annual Turnover:** ₹22,00,000 (₹10L domestic + ₹12L exports)
- Must register (exceeds ₹20 lakh threshold)
- Domestic invoices: 18% IGST or CGST+SGST
- Export invoices: 0% with LUT
- Claims ITC proportionately (based on taxable vs zero-rated turnover)

## 8. Common Mistakes Freelancers Make (and How to Avoid Them)

1. **Not registering when turnover crosses threshold** — Attracts 10% penalty on tax dues
2. **Forgetting to file LUT before exporting** — Results in mandatory IGST collection and complex refund claims
3. **Not issuing sequential invoices** — Creates discrepancies in GSTR-1 and can trigger scrutiny
4. **Claiming full ITC on assets used partly for personal use** — Only the business-use proportion is claimable
5. **Missing GSTR-3B filing deadline** — ₹50/day late fee (₹20 for NIL) plus 18% interest on dues

---

## Key Takeaways

- Register for GST once your annual service income crosses ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special states)
- Export services to foreign clients at 0% by filing an LUT — do not collect GST from them
- Claim ITC on all legitimate business purchases: laptops, software, internet, office rent
- Issue proper tax invoices within 30 days of service completion
- File GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B monthly — even if NIL — to avoid late fees

## GST on Gold in India: The Complete Tax Guide for 2026

Gold holds a unique cultural and financial significance in India. The country is one of the world's largest gold consumers, with demand driven by jewellery, investment, and religious ceremonies. Under the GST framework, gold occupies a special tax position — taxed at a unique 3% rate, distinct from the standard slabs of 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%.

This guide covers everything you need to know about GST on gold: the rates, how making charges are taxed, HSN codes, ITC rules, import duty structure, and how gold loans interact with GST.

## How GST is Calculated on a Gold Jewellery Purchase

When you buy gold jewellery from a jeweller, the invoice typically has two components:

1. **Gold value** (based on weight × gold rate per gram) → 3% GST
2. **Making charges** (labour for crafting) → 5% GST

**Example — Buying a Gold Necklace:**

| Component | Value | GST Rate | GST Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold (10g at ₹7,000/g) | ₹70,000 | 3% | ₹2,100 |
| Making charges | ₹5,000 | 5% | ₹250 |
| Hallmarking charges | ₹150 | 18% | ₹27 |
| **Total** | **₹75,150** | — | **₹2,377** |
| **Final Invoice** | **₹77,527** | — | — |

## Pre-GST vs Post-GST Gold Taxation

Before GST (pre-July 2017), gold was taxed under:
- **Excise Duty:** 1%
- **VAT:** 1–1.2% (varied by state)
- **Service Tax on making charges:** 12%
- **Total effective tax:** ~2–3%

After GST:
- **GST on gold value:** 3%
- **GST on making charges:** 5%
- **Net effect:** Slightly higher for high making-charge jewellery

## ITC Rules for Gold Jewellers

### Can Jewellers Claim ITC on Gold Purchases?
This depends on who the jeweller sells to:

| Scenario | ITC Eligibility |
|---|---|
| Retailer selling to end consumers (B2C) | **No ITC** on gold purchased |
| Manufacturer selling to retailers (B2B) | ITC available on gold purchased |
| Exporter selling to foreign buyers | Full ITC or refund (zero-rated) |

**Why no ITC for B2C?** The government restricts ITC for jewellers selling to end consumers to prevent tax evasion in the largely cash-based retail gold market.

### ITC on Business Expenses (Jewellers Can Claim)
Even without ITC on gold, jewellers can claim ITC on:
- Machinery and equipment used in jewellery making
- Shop rent (if GST charged by landlord)
- Professional services (CA fees, legal costs)
- Office equipment, computers, software

## GST on Gold Import into India

Imported gold faces a much higher tax burden than domestic purchases:

| Tax Component | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Customs Duty (BCD) | 15% | On CIF value |
| Agriculture Infrastructure Cess | 2.5% | On CIF value |
| IGST | 3% | On CIF + BCD + AIC |

**Example — Importing 100g of gold at ₹7,000/g:**
- Gold value (CIF) = ₹7,00,000
- BCD (15%) = ₹1,05,000
- AIC (2.5%) = ₹17,500
- IGST base = ₹8,22,500
- IGST (3%) = ₹24,675
- **Total landed cost = ₹8,47,175**

## GST on Gold Loans and Gold Leasing

### Gold Loans (NBFCs and Banks)
When you take a loan against gold jewellery:
- **Interest on gold loan:** Exempt from GST (financial service)
- **Processing fees:** 18% GST
- **Storage/safe-keeping charges:** 18% GST

### Gold Leasing (Mining/Jewellery Industry)
Gold leased by banks to jewellers for manufacturing:
- **Leasing fees:** 3% GST (treated as supply of gold)
- Bank leases gold → manufacturer makes jewellery → returns gold + leasing fees

## Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) on Gold

Under RCM, certain gold transactions require the **buyer** (rather than the seller) to pay GST directly to the government:

| Transaction | Who Pays GST |
|---|---|
| Unregistered jeweller selling to registered dealer | Registered dealer pays under RCM |
| Old gold (scrap) sold by unregistered person | Buyer pays under RCM |

## Key Takeaways for Gold Buyers and Jewellers

### For Consumers (Retail Buyers)
1. Pay **3% GST on gold value** + **5% GST on making charges**
2. Request a proper GST invoice with GSTIN, gold weight, rate, and GST breakup
3. Hallmarking charges (BIS) attract 18% GST — this is small but mandatory for BIS hallmarking

### For Jewellers and Businesses
1. B2C jewellers **cannot claim ITC** on gold purchases
2. B2B manufacturers/exporters **can claim ITC** on gold
3. Gold exchange/buy-back is taxed at 3% on the transaction value
4. Maintain proper purchase records for audit trails

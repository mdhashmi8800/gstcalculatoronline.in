## Key Changes in Indian GST Laws (2025–2026)
The Goods and Services Tax framework is highly dynamic. The GST Council, headed by the Union Finance Minister and comprising finance representatives of all states, meets periodically to revise rates, update compliance filing software, and adjust penalty guidelines. Below is a detailed summary of the latest amendments.

## 1. Rationalization of GSTR-3B Late Fee Caps
To provide relief to small tax entities, the late fee structure under Section 47 of the CGST Act has been rationalized. The maximum fee cap for late filings is now linked directly to the taxpayer's **Aggregate Annual Turnover (AATO)**:
*   **NIL Return Filers:** Capped at **₹500** per return (₹250 CGST + ₹250 SGST).
*   **AATO up to ₹1.5 Crore:** Capped at **₹2,000** per return (₹1,000 CGST + ₹1,000 SGST).
*   **AATO between ₹1.5 Crore and ₹5 Crore:** Capped at **₹5,000** per return (₹2,500 CGST + ₹2,500 SGST).
*   **AATO above ₹5 Crore:** Standard late fee of ₹50/day up to a maximum cap of **₹10,000**.

---

## 2. E-Invoicing Threshold Extensions
E-invoicing under GST is being systematically expanded. It has been made mandatory for businesses with an aggregate annual turnover exceeding **₹5 Crore** in any previous financial year. E-invoices must be generated directly through the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) to facilitate automated ITC matching on GSTR-2B.

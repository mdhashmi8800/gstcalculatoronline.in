## Key Changes in Indian GST Laws (2025–2026)

The Goods and Services Tax framework is highly dynamic. The GST Council — headed by the Union Finance Minister and comprising finance ministers of all states — meets periodically to revise tax rates, update compliance procedures, and adjust penalty structures. This article consolidates the most significant changes from the past 12 months that affect businesses, CAs, and taxpayers filing returns.

> **Last Updated:** July 18, 2026. We update this article within 15 business days of each GST Council meeting.

---

## 1. Rationalized GSTR-3B Late Fee Structure

One of the most impactful compliance changes in recent years has been the rationalization of late fees under Section 47 of the CGST Act. The GST Council introduced turnover-based maximum caps to provide relief to small taxpayers:

| Taxpayer Category | Daily Late Fee | Maximum Cap |
|---|---|---|
| NIL Return Filers (zero tax liability) | ₹20/day | ₹500 per return |
| AATO up to ₹1.5 Crore | ₹50/day | ₹2,000 per return |
| AATO ₹1.5 Crore to ₹5 Crore | ₹50/day | ₹5,000 per return |
| AATO above ₹5 Crore | ₹50/day | ₹10,000 per return |

> **AATO** = Aggregate Annual Turnover in the preceding financial year.

## 2. E-Invoicing Threshold Expansions

E-invoicing under GST has been progressively rolled out by lowering the turnover threshold:

| Period | Mandatory for Businesses with AATO > |
|---|---|
| October 2020 | ₹500 Crore |
| January 2021 | ₹100 Crore |
| April 2021 | ₹50 Crore |
| April 2022 | ₹20 Crore |
| October 2022 | ₹10 Crore |
| August 2023 | ₹5 Crore |

**Current status (2026):** All businesses with AATO above ₹5 Crore must generate e-invoices through the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP). The government has indicated further reduction but has not set a final date.

### Key E-Invoicing Compliance Points
- E-invoices must be generated via IRP (iris.einvoice1.gst.gov.in) or authorized GSP
- A unique IRN (Invoice Reference Number) and QR code are generated
- GSTR-1 is auto-populated from e-invoices, reducing manual errors
- Invalid e-invoices are not eligible for ITC by recipients

## 3. Section 43B(h) of Income Tax Act — GST-Linked Impact

**Effective from FY 2023-24:** Section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act now requires that payments to MSME vendors must be made within the time prescribed under the MSMED Act to be deductible. Payments beyond this period become disallowable in the year of payment.

**Impact on GST practitioners:** Businesses must now ensure MSME vendor payments are tracked alongside their GST compliance calendar to avoid income tax disallowances.

---

## 4. ITC Reversal Rules — GSTR-2B Matching

The GST portal has strengthened ITC matching through GSTR-2B:

### Key Rule Changes:
1. **ITC only available if reflected in GSTR-2B** — Provisional ITC credits have been gradually phased out
2. **Auto-reversal for mismatches** — If a supplier's GSTR-1 is filed late, ITC may not appear in GSTR-2B for that month and must be claimed the following month
3. **Rule 37A implementation** — If a supplier defaults on GSTR-3B payment, the recipient must reverse ITC until the supplier pays

### Practical Impact:
- Businesses must now review GSTR-2B carefully before filing GSTR-3B
- Follow up with suppliers who have not filed GSTR-1 on time
- Maintain invoice-level records for all ITC claims

---

## 5. GST Amnesty Scheme for Past Non-Filers

The GST Council periodically announces amnesty schemes for taxpayers who missed filing returns for previous financial years. Under the most recent scheme:

- **Eligibility:** GSTR-3B returns for specific tax periods that were not filed
- **Benefit:** Waiver of late fees beyond the standard cap for defaulters who file by the amnesty deadline
- **Interest:** Full interest at 18% p.a. on net outstanding tax is still applicable

> Check the official GST Portal (gst.gov.in) for the current amnesty scheme notification dates.

---

## 6. GSTR-1A — New Amendment Return

The CBIC introduced **GSTR-1A** to allow taxpayers to amend B2B invoices already reported in GSTR-1 before the GSTR-3B due date:

- Addresses errors in invoice details, GSTIN, taxable value, or tax amounts
- Available only until GSTR-3B is filed for that month
- Particularly useful for: wrong GSTIN in invoice, incorrect HSN codes, or taxable value corrections

## 7. GST Rate Changes — Recent Revisions

### Rate Reductions (Consumer Relief)
- **Uncooked/unfried snack pellets:** Reduced from 18% to 5%
- **Printing/drawing ink:** Reduced from 18% to 12%

### Rate Increases (Revenue)
- **Online gaming services (real money):** Now subject to 28% GST on full face value of bets
- **Casino entries and horse racing:** 28% GST on the full bet amount

### Clarifications
- **Healthcare services by hospitals:** Confirmed exempt
- **Millet-based food products:** Reduced to 0% for unbranded; 5% for branded

## 8. Key Compliance Deadlines for FY 2026-27

| Return / Activity | Due Date | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| GSTR-1 (monthly filers) | 11th of following month | Monthly |
| GSTR-1 (quarterly, QRMP) | 13th of following month after quarter | Quarterly |
| GSTR-3B (monthly, >₹5 Crore) | 20th of following month | Monthly |
| GSTR-3B (monthly, ₹1.5-5 Crore) | 22nd/24th of following month | Monthly |
| GSTR-3B (QRMP scheme) | 22nd/24th of following month after quarter | Quarterly |
| GSTR-9 (annual return) | 31st December | Annual |
| GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement) | 31st December | Annual (if AATO > ₹5 Crore) |
| E-ITC ledger reconciliation | Before filing GSTR-3B | Monthly |

## 9. Upcoming Changes Expected in 2026

Based on GST Council deliberations and budget announcements, the following changes are expected:

1. **Real estate GTA services:** Possible GST rationalization for goods transport agencies
2. **GSTIN-linked bank accounts:** Integration of banking data with GSTIN for better compliance tracking
3. **Faceless assessments:** Expansion of AI-driven faceless scrutiny assessments
4. **Further e-invoicing threshold reduction:** Likely to come down to ₹1 Crore or ₹2 Crore

## Key Takeaways

1. **Late fee caps** are now turnover-linked — small businesses pay a maximum of ₹2,000 per delayed GSTR-3B
2. **E-invoicing mandatory** for AATO > ₹5 Crore — non-compliance can disqualify ITC at recipient end
3. **GSTR-2B is now the definitive ITC source** — provisional credits have been phased out
4. **Online gaming at 28%** on full face value of bets is now firmly established law
5. **GSTR-1A** allows pre-GSTR-3B corrections to GSTR-1 invoice data

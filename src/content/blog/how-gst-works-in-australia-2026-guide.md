## What Is GST in Australia?

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a broad-based consumption tax added to most goods and services sold in Australia. It's been in place since 1 July 2000, and it works as a multi-stage tax — businesses collect it from customers and pass it on to the Australian Taxation Office (ATO), while also claiming back GST they've paid on business purchases (called input tax credits).

Think of your business as a tax collector working on behalf of the ATO. You add GST to your sale price, hold onto it briefly, then hand it over — minus whatever GST you've already paid on your own expenses.

## Current GST Rate (2026)

The standard GST rate in Australia is **10%**, applied to most goods and services. There's also a reduced 5% rate that applies to a narrow set of items like domestic fuel, water and sewerage services, children's car seats, and certain medical aids for people with disabilities.

| Rate | Applies To |
|------|-----------|
| 10% (Standard) | Most goods and services |
| 5% (Reduced) | Domestic fuel, water/sewerage, children's car seats, some medical aids |
| 0% (GST-free) | Basic food, exports, most medical/health services, education |

## Who Needs to Register for GST?

You must register for GST if:

- Your business's **GST turnover reaches $75,000 or more** in any 12-month period (current or projected)
- You run a **non-profit organisation** with turnover of $150,000 or more
- You're a **taxi, limousine, or rideshare driver** (Uber, Ola, DiDi, etc.) — regardless of how much you earn. There's no minimum threshold for this category.

You can also register **voluntarily** even if you're under the threshold — useful if your clients are GST-registered businesses, or you have significant GST-included expenses you want to claim back.

## The $75,000 Registration Threshold Explained

This is where most small business owners get tripped up. The threshold isn't based on your total revenue or profit — it's based on your **GST turnover**, which includes taxable and GST-free sales but excludes input-taxed supplies like residential rent or most financial income (interest, dividends).

**Example:** Priya runs an online store from Perth. Her sales for the past 12 months hit $80,000. Because she's crossed the $75,000 mark, she has **21 days** from that point to register for GST with the ATO.

Once registered, she must add 10% GST to her prices going forward and report it through her Business Activity Statement (BAS).

## How to Calculate GST (With Examples)

Because GST is calculated as 1/11th of the GST-inclusive price, the math is simple once you know the rate.

**Adding GST (GST-exclusive → GST-inclusive):**
Price × 1.10 = GST-inclusive price

*Example:* A product priced at $100 (excl. GST) → $100 × 1.10 = **$110** (incl. GST). The GST component is $10.

**Removing GST (GST-inclusive → GST-exclusive):**
Price ÷ 1.10 = GST-exclusive price

*Example:* An item sells for $110 (incl. GST) → $110 ÷ 1.10 = **$100** (excl. GST). GST portion = $110 ÷ 11 = $10.

| Calculation | Formula | Example |
|------------|---------|---------|
| Add GST | Price × 1.10 | $100 → $110 |
| Remove GST | Price ÷ 1.10 | $110 → $100 |
| Find GST portion | Price ÷ 11 | $110 → $10 |

## GST-Free vs Input-Taxed Supplies

This distinction confuses a lot of business owners, so here's a clear breakdown:

| Type | What It Means | Examples |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **GST-Free** | 0% GST charged, but you *can* claim GST credits on related purchases | Basic food, exports, most education and medical services |
| **Input-Taxed** | No GST charged, and you *cannot* claim GST credits | Residential rent, financial services (interest, most bank fees) |
| **Taxable** | Standard 10% GST applies | Most goods and services |

Getting this wrong can either cost you unclaimed credits or land you with compliance issues — worth double-checking with a registered tax agent if your business straddles multiple categories.

## GST on Imports and Digital Sales

GST also applies to low-value imported goods (under $1,000) sold to Australian consumers, and to digital products and services — think software subscriptions, streaming services, or e-books sold by overseas businesses to Australian customers. Foreign businesses selling into Australia above the threshold may need to register under the simplified GST system for non-residents.

## Filing GST: The BAS Process

Once registered, you'll typically report and pay GST through a **Business Activity Statement (BAS)**, usually lodged monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on your turnover. Your BAS shows:

- GST collected on sales (output tax)
- GST paid on purchases (input tax credits)
- The net amount owed to, or refundable from, the ATO

## Penalties for Late Registration

If you don't register on time, the ATO can backdate your GST liability to when you should have registered — meaning you may owe GST on sales you didn't actually collect it for, plus interest and potential penalties. It's a costly mistake that's entirely avoidable by tracking your turnover regularly.

## Is the Threshold About to Change?

As of 2026, a proposal has been put forward to raise the GST registration threshold from $75,000 to $250,000, aimed at reducing compliance burden on small businesses. This has **not yet been enacted** — the current threshold remains $75,000 ($150,000 for non-profits). Business owners should keep an eye on official ATO announcements rather than acting on the proposal prematurely.

## FAQs

**1. What is the current GST rate in Australia in 2026?**
The standard rate is 10%, with a reduced 5% rate for select items like domestic fuel and children's car seats.

**2. Do I have to register for GST if my turnover is below $75,000?**
Not usually — registration is optional below the threshold, except for taxi and rideshare drivers, who must register regardless of income.

**3. What items are GST-free in Australia?**
Most basic food, exports, and many medical and education services are GST-free, meaning 0% GST is charged but input tax credits can still be claimed.

**4. How do I calculate GST on a sale price?**
Multiply the GST-exclusive price by 1.10 to add GST, or divide a GST-inclusive price by 1.10 to remove it.

**5. What happens if I don't register for GST on time?**
The ATO can backdate your GST liability and apply penalties and interest, so it's important to register within 21 days of crossing the threshold.

**6. Is the GST threshold about to change?**
A proposal to raise it to $250,000 is under discussion in 2026, but as of now, the threshold remains $75,000.

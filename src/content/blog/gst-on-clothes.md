## GST on Clothing and Textiles: The Complete Guide for 2026

India's textile and garment industry is one of the largest in the world, employing millions across the value chain — from cotton farmers to weavers, dyers, tailors, and retailers. GST applies across this entire chain, with rates varying based on the type of product, its price point, and the stage of processing.

This guide covers every major GST rate applicable to the textile and clothing sector, including the key changes introduced in 2022 that removed the pricing threshold for fabric and yarn.

---

## GST Rates on Readymade Garments (Chapter 61 & 62)

The GST rate on readymade garments depends primarily on the **selling price per piece:**

| Garment Price per Piece | GST Rate | CGST | SGST | Quick Tool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹1,000 | 5% | 2.5% | 2.5% | [5% GST Calculator](/5-percent-gst-calculator) |
| Above ₹1,000 | 12% | 6% | 6% | [12% GST Calculator](/12-percent-gst-calculator) |

You can also use our master [Online GST Calculator](/gst-calculator) or check the official [GST Rates 2026 Directory](/gst-rates) for updated slab notifications.

**Effective Since:** January 1, 2022 (following the 46th GST Council meeting).

### Important: The "Branded vs Unbranded" Rule Has Changed
Before 2017, there was a distinction between branded and unbranded garments for central excise purposes. Under GST, **all readymade garments** (branded and unbranded) follow the same rate structure based on selling price. There is no longer any brand-based exemption.

## GST Rates on Fabrics and Textiles

| Fabric Type | HSN Chapter | GST Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Silk yarn and fabrics | Chapter 50 | 5% |
| Raw cotton (not carded/combed) | 5201 | 5% |
| Cotton yarn (not retail packs) | 5205–5207 | 12% |
| Cotton fabrics (woven) | 5208–5212 | 5% |
| Man-made filament yarn | 5402–5406 | 12% |
| Man-made synthetic fabrics | 5512–5516 | 5% |
| Wool yarn | 5106–5110 | 5% |
| Woollen fabrics | 5111–5113 | 5% |
| Jute fabrics | 5310 | 5% |
| Linen (flax) yarn and fabrics | Chapter 53 | 5% |

> **Note:** Following the January 2022 GST Council revision, most fabric and yarn now attract 5% GST across the board, eliminating the earlier 0% category for raw fabrics.

## GST on Specific Clothing Categories

### 1. Cotton T-Shirts, Shirts, and Trousers
- HSN: 6105 (knitted shirts), 6206 (woven shirts for women)
- GST: 5% (if price ≤ ₹1,000) / 12% (if price > ₹1,000)

### 2. Denim Jeans
- HSN: 6203 (men's trousers) / 6204 (women's)
- GST: 12% (most denim jeans price > ₹1,000)

### 3. Sarees
- HSN: 5208 to 5212 (cotton sarees), 5407 (silk/synthetic sarees)
- Unstitched fabric: 5% GST
- Stitched/readymade saree blouse: Charged as garment (5% or 12% based on price)

### 4. Woollen Sweaters and Winter Wear
- HSN: 6110 (knitted jerseys, sweaters)
- GST: 5% (if price ≤ ₹1,000) / 12% (if price > ₹1,000)

### 5. Children's Clothing
- HSN: 6111 (knitted baby clothes), 6209 (woven baby clothes)
- GST: Same rate structure — 5% up to ₹1,000, 12% above ₹1,000

### 6. Sportswear and Activewear
- HSN: 6211
- GST: 5% (if price ≤ ₹1,000) / 12% (if price > ₹1,000)

### 7. Handloom Products
- Handloom sarees, dhotis, lungis: 5% GST
- Handloom carpets: 5% GST
- Khadi: 0% if sold through KVIC outlets (specific exemption)

## GST Input Tax Credit (ITC) in the Textile Chain

The textile supply chain is long: cotton farm → ginning → spinning → weaving → dyeing → garment manufacturing → wholesale → retail. GST allows each B2B participant to claim ITC:

| Stage | Buys at | ITC on Purchase | Sells at | Output Tax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spinning mill | Raw cotton (5%) | Claims ₹5,000 ITC | Yarn (12%) | Pays ₹12,000 |
| Weaving unit | Yarn (12%) | Claims ₹12,000 ITC | Fabric (5%) | Pays ₹5,000 (ITC > output = refund claim) |
| Garment maker | Fabric (5%) | Claims ₹5,000 ITC | Garment (12%) | Pays ₹12,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹7,000 net |
| Retailer | Garment (12%) | Claims ₹12,000 ITC | To consumer (12%) | Pays net difference |

> **Inverted Duty Structure Problem:** Weaving units buying yarn at 12% but selling fabric at 5% accumulate excess ITC. They can claim a refund of this accumulated ITC using our [GST Refund Calculator](/gst-refund-calculator).

## GST on Import of Clothing

Imported garments attract:
1. **Basic Customs Duty (BCD):** 20% on CIF value
2. **Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS):** 10% of BCD
3. **IGST:** 12% on (CIF + BCD + SWS) (compute cross-border taxes with our [IGST Calculator](/igst-calculator))

**Example:** Importing a shirt valued at ₹2,000:
- BCD = 20% × ₹2,000 = ₹400
- SWS = 10% × ₹400 = ₹40
- Assessable Value = ₹2,000 + ₹400 + ₹40 = ₹2,440
- IGST = 12% × ₹2,440 = ₹292.80
- **Total landed cost = ₹2,732.80**

## Key Takeaways for Textile Businesses

1. **Garments ≤ ₹1,000/piece:** Charge 5% GST (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST or 5% IGST)
2. **Garments > ₹1,000/piece:** Charge 12% GST (6% CGST + 6% SGST or 12% IGST)
3. **Most fabrics:** 5% GST across the board (post-Jan 2022 revision)
4. **Yarn:** 12% GST for most man-made and cotton yarn
5. **Weaving units with inverted duty:** File for accumulated ITC refund using Rule 89

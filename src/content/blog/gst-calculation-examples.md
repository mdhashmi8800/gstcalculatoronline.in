## Overview of GST Billing Scenarios
Correct billing is the foundation of tax compliance in India. To ensure your tax invoices match the GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filings, you must understand when to split tax into CGST/SGST and when to charge the single combined IGST. The rule depends on the Place of Supply rules.

## Scenario 1: Local Intra-State Transaction (Within the same state)
When the seller's business location and the buyer's place of supply are in the same state, CGST and SGST are levied.

### Invoicing Steps:
1.  Enter the pre-tax taxable value of goods or services.
2.  Determine the combined tax slab (e.g., 18%).
3.  Calculate the total GST: Taxable Value × 18%.
4.  Divide that GST amount equally into Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST) lines.

*Example:* Selling ₹20,000 worth of leather shoes locally in Karnataka at 12% GST:
*   Taxable value: ₹20,000
*   CGST (6%): ₹1,200
*   SGST (6%): ₹1,200
*   Grand Total Invoice: ₹22,400

---

## Scenario 2: Interstate Transaction (Between different states)
When the seller is in one state and the place of supply is in another state, CGST and SGST do not apply. Instead, you levy Integrated GST (IGST) at the full combined slab rate.

### Invoicing Steps:
1.  Enter the pre-tax taxable base value.
2.  Levy IGST at the full slab percentage (e.g., 18%).
3.  Do not split the tax; the entire tax amount is declared under the IGST column.

*Example:* An IT firm in Noida (Uttar Pradesh) provides software consulting worth ₹1,00,000 to a client in Mumbai (Maharashtra) at 18% GST:
*   Taxable value: ₹1,00,000
*   IGST (18%): ₹18,000
*   Grand Total Invoice: ₹1,18,000

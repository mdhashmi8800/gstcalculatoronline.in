Running a commercial enterprise or professional practice in India requires navigating an intricate matrix of indirect tax regulations. With strict digital validation engines implemented across the **GST Common Portal**, inadvertent filing delays or calculation discrepancies trigger automatic scrutiny notices under Section 61, blocking of electronic credit ledgers under Rule 86A, and compounding interest liabilities under Section 50.

To safeguard your organization against audits, penalties, and blocked working capital, our indirect tax advisory team has engineered this **Comprehensive GST Compliance Checklist for 2026**. Designed for Chief Financial Officers, practicing Chartered Accountants, in-house accountants, and business owners, this guide details every recurring statutory milestone across monthly, quarterly, and annual operational cycles.

## 1. Monthly Compliance Calendar & Deadlines

Every regular taxpayer enrolled under the standard GST scheme must follow a structured monthly calendar:

| Timeline | Compliance Activity | Relevant Form | Purpose / Legal Provision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st – 10th of Month** | Outward Sales Ledger Reconciliation | Internal Accounting ERP | Audit all B2B and B2C sales invoices, credit notes, and debit notes generated during the preceding calendar month. |
| **11th of Month** | GSTR-1 Outward Supply Return | Form GSTR-1 | Mandatory deadline for monthly filers to upload all B2B invoices, export details, credit/debit notes, and B2C summaries. |
| **13th of Month** | Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF) | IFF (Optional for QRMP) | QRMP quarterly filers upload B2B invoices up to ₹50 Lakhs per month to pass on ITC to corporate buyers. |
| **14th of Month** | GSTR-2B Auto-Drafted ITC Lock | Form GSTR-2B | Download the static, legally binding monthly ITC statement generated automatically by the GSTN system. |
| **14th – 19th of Month** | Purchase Reconciliation & Matching | Internal ERP vs GSTR-2B | Reconcile purchase registers against GSTR-2B. Flag missing vendor invoices and communicate mismatches immediately. |
| **20th of Month** | GSTR-3B Summary Return & Tax Payment | Form GSTR-3B | Discharge net cash tax liabilities after utilizing eligible ITC. Final deadline for standard monthly filers. |
| **25th of Month** | PMT-06 Monthly Tax Deposit | Form GST PMT-06 | QRMP quarterly filers must deposit estimated monthly cash tax using either the Fixed Sum Method (35%) or Self-Assessment Method. |

## 2. Input Tax Credit (ITC) Verification & Safeguard Checklist

Maximizing Input Tax Credit while maintaining statutory safety is the most critical component of GST compliance. Under **Section 16(2)** and associated procedural rules, verify the following prior to submitting Form GSTR-3B:

### The 4 Pillars of ITC Eligibility (Section 16(2))
Before claiming any ITC in Table 4 of GSTR-3B, confirm that:
- [x] **Possession of Valid Tax Invoice:** You possess a statutory tax invoice, debit note, or bill of entry meeting all criteria specified in Rule 46.
- [x] **Receipt of Goods or Services:** The underlying goods or services have been physically delivered or rendered (or constructive receipt under Section 16(2)(b)).
- [x] **Tax Discharged by Vendor:** The supplier has reported the supply in their Form GSTR-1 and the invoice appears in your static Form GSTR-2B statement.
- [x] **Return Submitted:** You are furnishing a valid return under Section 39 (Form GSTR-3B).

### Proviso to Section 16(2): 180-Day Vendor Payment Rule
Under the second proviso to Section 16(2) read with **Rule 37**:
- Track all purchase invoices reaching the **180-day aging threshold**.
- If full consideration and tax remain unpaid after 180 days from the invoice date, the corresponding ITC **must be reversed** in Table 4(B)(2) of GSTR-3B along with interest at 18% p.a. under Section 50.
- Once the vendor is subsequently paid, the reversed ITC can be legally reclaimed in Table 4(A)(5) without any limitation period.

### Blocked Credits (Section 17(5))
Ensure zero ineligible ITC is claimed under negative list categories:
- Motor vehicles for transport of persons with seating capacity $\le 13$ persons (unless engaged in further supply, transportation of passengers, or driving training).
- Food, beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, and health services (unless used as an inward composite supply).
- Membership of clubs, health and fitness centers.
- Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation.
- Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, or disposed of by way of gift or free samples.
- Works contract services for construction of an immovable property (except plant and machinery or where inward works contract is used for outward works contract).

## 3. Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) Checklist

Under **Section 9(3) and Section 9(4)** of the CGST Act, certain inward supplies mandate tax payment directly by the recipient:

- [x] **Goods Transport Agency (GTA) Services:** Verify if the transporter has opted for forward charge (at 5% or 12%). If GTA has not issued a tax invoice under forward charge, pay 5% GST under RCM (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST).
- [x] **Legal Services from Advocates:** Fees paid to individual advocates, senior advocates, or firm of advocates for representational or legal advisory services attract 18% GST under RCM.
- [x] **Director Remuneration:** Sitting fees, commissions, or non-salary remuneration paid to independent or nominee directors attract 18% GST under RCM.
- [x] **Security Services:** Security personnel services supplied by any person other than a body corporate to a registered entity attract 18% RCM.
- [x] **Commercial Property Rent from Unregistered Persons:** Following recent GST Council rationalizations, rent paid on commercial real estate to unregistered landlords falls under RCM.
- [x] **Self-Invoicing Requirement (Section 31(3)(f)):** A registered recipient liable to pay tax under RCM on inward supplies from an unregistered person must issue a **Payment Voucher** and a **Self-Invoice**.

> [!TIP]
> Tax under RCM cannot be discharged by utilizing existing Input Tax Credit. It must be paid **strictly in cash** through the Electronic Cash Ledger. Once paid in cash, the taxpayer can immediately claim 100% of that RCM tax as eligible ITC in the same month's GSTR-3B return.

## 4. Quarterly & Annual Compliance Milestones

Beyond the monthly cadence, finance teams must execute periodic quarterly and annual audits:

### Quarterly Protocols
- **CMP-08 for Composition Taxpayers:** File Statement CMP-08 and pay flat-rate composition tax (1% for manufacturers/traders, 5% for restaurants, 6% for service providers) by the **18th of the month** following the quarter end.
- **GSTR-4 for Composition Filers:** Annual compilation filed by **30th April** following the end of the financial year.
- **QRMP Scheme GSTR-3B:** Quarterly filers submit complete return by the **22nd or 24th** of the month following the quarter end (staggered by state clusters).

### Annual Statutory Reconciliations (Section 44)
- **Form GSTR-9 (Annual Return):** Due by **31st December** of the subsequent financial year. Exempted for taxpayers whose aggregate annual turnover is up to ₹2 Crore.
- **Form GSTR-9C (Reconciliation Statement):** Self-certified reconciliation statement comparing audited financial statements (Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet) against figures furnished in GSTR-9. Mandatory for businesses with aggregate turnover exceeding **₹5 Crore**.
- **Cut-off for Prior Financial Year Adjustments:** Any unclaimed ITC, credit notes, or turnover adjustments relating to a financial year must be finalized on or before **30th November** following the end of that financial year (as amended by Finance Act 2022).

## 5. Books of Accounts & Record Retention (Section 35 & 36)

The CGST Act enforces stringent documentation rules. Businesses must maintain accurate and true physical or digital records at their principal place of business:

1. **Mandatory Registers to Maintain:**
   - Production or manufacture of goods register
   - Inward and outward supply register (Purchase and Sales books)
   - Stock register of inventory (opening balance, receipts, supplies, lost/destroyed stock, closing balance)
   - Input tax credit availed register
   - Output tax payable and discharged register
   - Goods and Services Tax Electronic Cash and Credit Ledger records
2. **Mandatory Retention Period (Section 36):**
   - Every registered taxpayer must preserve books of accounts and related tax invoices, vouchers, and contracts for **at least 72 months (6 years)** from the due date of furnishing the annual return for the year to which such records pertain.
   - If an enterprise is subject to an ongoing appeal, revision, or litigation before an Appellate Authority or Court, records must be retained for **one year after final disposal** of such proceedings.

## 6. Pre-Filing Review Protocol: Final 5-Minute Checklist

Before clicking the final "File GSTR-3B with EVC / DSC" button on the GST Common Portal, verify:

1. **Net Tax Cash Outflow:** Does the cash ledger debit match your internal financial forecasts?
2. **Reverse Charge Segregation:** Has RCM liability been recorded in Table 3.1(d) and paid exclusively through cash?
3. **ITC Reversal Reconciliation:** Have permanent reversals (Rule 38/42/43) and temporary 180-day vendor reversals been entered in Table 4(B)?
4. **HSN Summary Validation:** Does the total taxable value in Table 12 of GSTR-1 match the total turnover reported in Table 4, 5, and 6?
5. **No Negative Values in GSTR-3B:** Ensure no credit note adjustment creates an unallowable negative balance in tax tables.
